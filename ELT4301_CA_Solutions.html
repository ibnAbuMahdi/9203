Q 1b

FM and PM Signal Derivations

General Angle-Modulated Signal

An angle-modulated signal has the general form:

$$s(t) = A_c \cos\bigl[\,\theta_i(t)\,\bigr]$$

where the instantaneous frequency is defined as:

$$f_i(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\,\theta_i(t)}{dt}$$

â‘  Frequency Modulation (FM)

In FM, the instantaneous frequency varies linearly with the message \(m(t)\):

$$f_i(t) = f_c + k_f\,m(t)$$

Integrating to obtain \(\theta_i(t)\):

$$\theta_i(t) = 2\pi f_c t + 2\pi k_f \int_0^t m(\tau)\,d\tau$$

FM Signal Expression:

$$\boxed{s_{\mathrm{FM}}(t) = A_c \cos\!\left[2\pi f_c t + 2\pi k_f \int_0^t m(\tau)\,d\tau\right]}$$

For single-tone \(m(t) = A_m\cos(2\pi f_m t)\):

$$s_{\mathrm{FM}}(t) = A_c \cos\!\bigl[2\pi f_c t + \beta_{\mathrm{FM}}\sin(2\pi f_m t)\bigr]$$

FM Modulation Index:

$$\boxed{\beta_{\mathrm{FM}} = \frac{\Delta f}{f_m} = \frac{k_f A_m}{f_m}}$$

â‘¡ Phase Modulation (PM)

In PM, the instantaneous phase varies linearly with the message \(m(t)\):

$$\theta_i(t) = 2\pi f_c t + k_p\,m(t)$$

Substituting directly into the general form:

 

PM Signal Expression:

$$\boxed{s_{\mathrm{PM}}(t) = A_c \cos\!\bigl[2\pi f_c t + k_p\,m(t)\bigr]}$$

For single-tone \(m(t) = A_m\cos(2\pi f_m t)\):

$$s_{\mathrm{PM}}(t) = A_c \cos\!\bigl[2\pi f_c t + \beta_{\mathrm{PM}}\cos(2\pi f_m t)\bigr]$$

PM Modulation Index:

$$\boxed{\beta_{\mathrm{PM}} = k_p A_m}$$

AM Signal Analysis

Given: Peak-to-peak envelope = 200 V  |  Trough-to-trough envelope = 40 V

Fig 3 - AM Signal with Envelope Annotations

Step 1 - Identify Vmax and Vmin

AM signal: \(s(t) = [A_c + A_m\cos(2\pi f_m t)]\cos(2\pi f_c t)\)

\(2V_{\max} = 200\,\text{V} \;\Rightarrow\; V_{\max} = 100\,\text{V} = A_c + A_m\)
\(2V_{\min} = 40\,\text{V} \;\Rightarrow\; V_{\min} = 20\,\text{V} = A_c - A_m\)
Step 2 - Solve for Ac and Am $$A_c = \frac{V_{\max}+V_{\min}}{2} = \frac{100+20}{2} = \mathbf{60\,V}$$ $$A_m = \frac{V_{\max}-V_{\min}}{2} = \frac{100-20}{2} = \mathbf{40\,V}$$
Step 3 - Modulation Depth $$\mu = \frac{A_m}{A_c} = \frac{40}{60} = 0.667 \quad (66.7\,\%)$$
