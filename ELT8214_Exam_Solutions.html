Question 1 (a) & (b)

Information Measure, Entropy, Channel Capacity & Transition Diagrams

Part 1 (a)(i): Shannon's Information Measure \(I(X)\) and Entropy \(H(X)\)

For a discrete memoryless source emitting symbol \(x_i\) with probability \(P(x_i)\):

  • Self-Information Measure \(I(x_i)\):
    $$I(x_i) = \log_b\left(\frac{1}{P(x_i)}\right) = -\log_b P(x_i)$$
    When base \(b = 2\), the unit is bits (or Shannons). When \(b = e\), the unit is nats. When \(b = 10\), the unit is Hartleys (decits).
  • Source Entropy \(H(X)\): Defined as the mathematical expectation (statistical average) of the self-information over all \(M\) possible symbols:
    $$H(X) = E\bigl[I(X)\bigr] = \sum_{i=1}^{M} P(x_i)\,I(x_i) = -\sum_{i=1}^{M} P(x_i)\log_2 P(x_i) \quad \text{(bits/symbol)}$$

Binary Source Curves: \(I(p)\) and \(H(p)\)

Fig 1: Self-Information \(I(p) = -\log_2(p)\) and Binary Entropy Function \(H(p) = -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p)\)

Key Curve Observations:
  • \(I(p)\): Monotonically decreases from \(\infty\) as \(p \to 0\) down to \(0\) at \(p = 1\).
  • \(H(p)\): Strictly concave symmetric curve:
    • \(H(0) = 0\) (absolute certainty in symbol 0).
    • \(H(1) = 0\) (absolute certainty in symbol 1).
    • Maximum entropy: \(H(0.5) = 1.0\text{ bit/symbol}\) at equiprobable distribution \(p = 0.5\).

Part 1 (a)(ii): Three Intuitive Properties & Relationships with Uncertainty

  1. Non-negativity: Since \(0 \le P(x_i) \le 1\), \(\frac{1}{P(x_i)} \ge 1\), hence \(I(x_i) \ge 0\). Receipt of an event can never decrease our knowledge or convey negative information.
  2. Monotonicity with Probability / Uncertainty: If \(P(x_1) < P(x_2)\), then \(I(x_1) > I(x_2)\). Less probable events represent higher surprise/uncertainty, thus conveying greater information upon realization. A certain event (\(P=1\)) yields \(I = 0\).
  3. Additivity for Independent Events: If two occurrences \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) are statistically independent, \(P(x_1, x_2) = P(x_1)P(x_2)\), and: $$I(x_1, x_2) = -\log_2[P(x_1)P(x_2)] = -\log_2 P(x_1) - \log_2 P(x_2) = I(x_1) + I(x_2)$$
Three Relationships between \(I(X)\) and Uncertainty:
  1. Information gained is directly equal to the amount of uncertainty resolved by the occurrence of the event (\(\Delta U = I\)).
  2. Maximum uncertainty in a source occurs when all outcomes are equiprobable, which simultaneously maximizes the average information (entropy \(H_{max} = \log_2 M\)).
  3. Zero uncertainty implies zero information (\(P=1 \implies I=0\)); infinite uncertainty as an event becomes vanishingly rare implies unbounded information (\(P \to 0 \implies I \to \infty\)).

Part 1 (b)(i): Channel Capacity

For a discrete memoryless channel with input alphabet \(X\) and output alphabet \(Y\), the transmission of information is quantified by the Mutual Information \(I(X; Y)\):

$$I(X; Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|X) \quad \text{(bits/channel use)}$$

Channel Capacity \(C\): Defined as the maximum mutual information over all possible input probability distributions \(P(X)\):

$$C = \max_{P(X)} I(X; Y) \quad \text{(bits/channel use)}$$

For a continuous-time, band-limited channel corrupted by Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN), the maximum information rate is given by the Shannon-Hartley Theorem:

$$\boxed{C = B \log_2\left(1 + \frac{S}{N}\right) = B \log_2\left(1 + \frac{S}{N_0 B}\right) \quad \text{(bits/second)}}$$

where \(B\) is the channel bandwidth (Hz), \(S\) is average signal power (W), \(N\) is total noise power (W), and \(N_0\) is noise power spectral density (W/Hz).

Question 5 (a) & (b)

Sampling, Quantization, Mutual Information & Code Classifications

Part 5 (a)(i): Sampling Theory & Sample-and-Hold Process

Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem:

A continuous-time band-limited signal \(x(t)\) with maximum frequency component \(f_{max} = W\text{ Hz}\) can be completely reconstructed from its samples without loss of information if sampled at a rate:

$$f_s \ge 2 W$$

where \(2W\) is the Nyquist rate and \(T_s = \frac{1}{f_s}\) is the Nyquist interval. If \(f_s < 2W\), spectral overlap known as aliasing occurs.

Fig 6: Sample and Hold (S/H) Circuit and Flat-Top PAM Generation

Sample and Hold (S/H) Operation:
  1. Sample Phase: An electronic switch (FET) closes during sampling pulse width \(\tau\). The holding capacitor \(C_h\) charges instantaneously to input voltage \(x(t)\).
  2. Hold Phase: Switch opens. Capacitor retains stored voltage across high input impedance buffer until next clock pulse.
  3. Output: Produces a flat-top staircase pulse sequence suitable for stable A/D conversion.

Part 5 (a)(ii): Quantization Process

Fig 7: Uniform Mid-Tread Quantizer Characteristic & Quantization Error

Quantization Principles:
  • Converts continuous-amplitude samples into discrete representation levels \(L = 2^n\).
  • Quantization Step Size: \(\Delta = \frac{V_{max} - V_{min}}{L}\).
  • Quantization Error: \(e_q = x_q - x\), bounded within \([-\Delta/2, +\Delta/2]\).
  • Quantization Noise Power: \(\sigma_q^2 = \frac{\Delta^2}{12}\).

Part 5 (b)(i): Conditional Entropy, Mutual Information & Venn Diagram

Mathematical Expressions:
  • Conditional Entropy (Equivocation): $$H(X|Y) = -\sum_{j}\sum_{i} P(x_i, y_j)\log_2 P(x_i | y_j) = H(X, Y) - H(Y)$$ $$H(Y|X) = -\sum_{i}\sum_{j} P(x_i, y_j)\log_2 P(y_j | x_i) = H(X, Y) - H(X)$$
  • Average Mutual Information \(I(X; Y)\): $$I(X; Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|X) = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)$$ $$I(X; Y) = \sum_{i}\sum_{j} P(x_i, y_j)\log_2\left(\frac{P(x_i, y_j)}{P(x_i)P(y_j)}\right)$$
